This continuation adds new record readings and closes a shared namesake lead. ## Sainte-Rosalie: the 1871 Pierre / Louise household is C233

The original **1871 census**, Québec, district 122 Bagot, subdistrict C Sainte-Rosalie, pages 36–37, dwelling 102 and corrected family 125, identifies **Pierre Gendron, 42, notaire**, with Louise, 44; Joseph, 19; Saül, 18; Célina, 16; Adéline, 14; Aimé, seven; Régina, six; and **Flavien Dupont, 24, étudiant en loi**. The family number 126 was struck out and replaced by 125. [Original 1871 image](https://data2.collectionscanada.gc.ca/1871/jpg/4395477_00467.jpg)

The original **1861 census** at Sainte-Rosalie, first sheet, lines 22–29, records **P. S. Gendron, 33, notaire**, **Louise Fournier, 34**, and Joseph, ten; Saül, nine; Célina, seven; and Adéline, five. Flavien Dupont, 14, is marked as a resident outside the family, as is the following Adèle, 15. The matching locality, occupation, four younger names in the same order with coherent ages, and recurring associate establish household continuity. The age columns use different conventions: 1861 asks age at next birthday; 1871 age at last birthday. Neither census provides the head's parents or a full set of kinship relationships. [Original 1861 image](https://data2.collectionscanada.gc.ca/1861/jpg/4108659_00181.jpg)

The saved **31 August 1828 baptism** and **13 May 1850 marriage** connect this household to **Pierre Samuel Gendron, C233**, son of Simon Gendron and Marie Louise Yon, husband of Louise Fournier. The marriage's full given name is Pierre Samuel across a line break; its faint occupation is now cautiously read **[instituteur ?]**, replacing the earlier cultivateur reading. Its faded maternal surname remains explicitly uncertain. [C233's original-source transcriptions and census comparison](https://vetharmon.github.io/gendron-research/candidates/c233/)

This closes the specifically inspected Sainte-Rosalie household as a lead for **C211, C183 and C281**. It does not establish those candidates' fates. C211 stays Needs work; C183 retains its separate qualified estate-based Likely eliminated assessment; C281 retains the unconfirmed Michigan Peter Jendro comparison. ## C181 / C042: a distinct 1824 infant and a carefully bounded sibling check

The Public Archives **later typed copy** of Saint-Clément, C-2889 image 275, page 267, records an **unnamed infant**, privately baptized at home, buried **7 July 1824**, son or daughter of Joseph Gendron and Marguerite Daigneau. The child died **avant-hier**, giving a calculated death date of **5 July**, aged **half an hour**. The act does not explicitly state the child's sex. This child died before Étienne's 1826 birth and cannot be the Joseph buried in January 1828. [Complete typed act](https://heritage.canadiana.ca/view/oocihm.lac_reel_c2889/275)

The already known **Joseph Jeandron baptism of 4 May 1806**, C-2891 image 902, page 1229, has now received a full French transcription and English translation. It gives birth the preceding day and parents Joseph Jeandron / Marguerite Deniau. The existing probable-sibling interpretation remains qualified; the rereading is not a newly discovered birth or an identified later adult. [Typed baptism](https://heritage.canadiana.ca/view/oocihm.lac_reel_c2891/902)

A further OCR-assisted screen of C-2889 images **200–354**, 155 frames, from **21 August 1822 through 25 March 1826**, found no separate same-parent Joseph baptism. Selected possible matches and both date boundaries were read visually; this is a bounded discovery result, not a complete manual negative. The contemporary **26 January 1828 burial manuscript** remains the decisive unresolved comparison for the Joseph / Étienne name and age conflict. Neither the new infant burial nor the screen establishes survival or death for Paul or Étienne.
