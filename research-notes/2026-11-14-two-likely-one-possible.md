## C033 — why likely

The original 29 January 1818 St-Roch-des-Aulnaies spread records Fabien, born the previous day, son of Jean Baptiste Gendreau, cordonnier, and Claire Caron. The adjoining baptism names twin Marie Marcelline, also born the previous day. LAFRANCE 2416218; FamilySearch DGS 5471267, image 293:
https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QSQ-G99W-FX3Q

The saved original 1881 census, East Northumberland 123, Murray, p. 36 lines 22–25, records Fabien Jandrew, 64, Québec-born, French, shoemaker, with Catherine 51, William 25 and Charles 11. The saved 1891 register image, East Northumberland 98, Murray, p. 5 lines 7–9, family 22, records Fabien **76**, Québec-born farmer, Catherine 61 and Charles 21. The enlarged age reads 76, not the previously logged 75. Those ages imply births about 1816/17 and 1814/15; they do not exactly match 1818. The 1891 survival, if the identification is accepted, excludes Franklin’s 1886 death.

Prior research inspected the complete account “This a Family write up,” story.txt, contributed by Joan H. King to FamilySearch L5DD-DB1, on 10 November 2026. It names the twin Marie Marcelline, brothers Piere and Jean Baptiste, St-Roch origin, shoemaking and Ontario migration. That inspection and its limits are recorded in the consolidated audit’s C033 resumption checkpoint and C147 archive findings. The memory text was not freshly reopened in this pass. The named twin plus original family/trade/place and adult census evidence make a likely identification reasonable. We do not treat the account as independent testimony whose origins have been established: its author and documentary basis are unknown and it may incorporate parish research. The census records do not name Fabien’s parents. The family worksheet’s 1816 France birth is contrary to the Québec birthplace in both census images and is retained as a conflicting secondary statement. No original adult marriage/death supplies a final parentage bridge.

Assessment: **likely**, not solid. The original 1891 image is held as a user-contributed scan; repository prose distinguishes that provenance from a fresh archive retrieval.

## C132 — why likely

Original St-Ours baptism, 27 November 1823, Gabriel born that morning, Pierre Gendron and Marie Charbonault. LAFRANCE 2632132; FamilySearch DGS 5471115, image 335:
https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-899Q-7SNQ

Original St-Jude baptism B2, 8 January 1826, Paul born yesterday, Pierre Gendron and Marie Charbonneau. LAFRANCE 4724655; FamilySearch DGS 5471045 image 215:
https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-L99Q-Q8NP

The shared February burial spread names Gilbert, about two, buried 7 February after dying two days earlier, and Paul, about one month, buried 9 February after dying yesterday. Both name Pierre Gendron and Marie Chardonais. LAFRANCE 4726112 and 4726113. Paul’s continuation was reread and adds witnesses, without a maternal alias. The parish copy is DGS 5471045, image 221, previously checked:
https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-899Q-QDFZ

The original parental marriage, St-Ours 26 July 1819, LAFRANCE 5728721, names Pierre Gendron and Marie Charbonault; it supports the baptismal surname but does not resolve Chardonais.

The two age-compatible children, the same father, repeated maternal surname in the paired burials and close burial dates support a probable family correlation. This is stronger than treating Gilbert’s burial in isolation. Gabriel was about two years two months; Paul was about one month. No claim is made that an epidemic or shared cause of death has been established.

**Unresolved:** Gabriel/Gilbert; Charbonault/Charbonneau versus Chardonais; incomplete reconstruction of any competing Pierre Gendron/Marie Chardonais family. No alias or correction has been found. Paul’s classification is not circular evidence for Gabriel: the baptism and burial details for both children are the evidence. Assessment changes to **likely**, not solid, on their combined weight.

## C106 — original marriage verified

Baptism B424, 11 November 1822, St-Hyacinthe, original spread d1p_03890461: Pierre born that day, Eustache Gendron, maître d’école du lieu, Josephte Lefebvre. Begins bottom left and continues top right. LAFRANCE 2478745.

Marriage M139, lower left, 13 May 1842, Notre-Dame Montréal, LAFRANCE 13401056: Pierre Gendron, resident Montréal, minor son of Eustache Gendron, cultivateur, and deceased Josephte Lefebvre, of St-Hyacinthe; bride Monique Huneau, adult daughter of Michel Huneau and Monique Longtin of St-Clément-de-Beauharnois. The closing text records the groom’s father’s consent. Both parents and parish connect him to the baptism; calculated age about nineteen and a half fits minor. Schoolteacher/farmer occupation variation remains visible.

This supersedes the old “original not inspected” statement. It establishes a useful candidate-to-spouse connection, but no post-1850 household or later death has been established. An 1842 marriage alone does not preclude a later Missouri identity after a spouse’s death or separation. Assessment: **possible elimination**. PRDH profile 798111/family 219181 are finding aids, and their empty child list is not proof of childlessness.
