# Châteauguay families: later-life tracing — 21 November 2026

## Assessment

The third pass adds two substantial later-life findings. An original census places **Gatien Martin and Catherine Gendron at Saint-Timothée in 1851**, with a family matching the known couple. A contemporary newspaper and Michigan’s official printed service book identify **Paul Jondro/Jondrieux of Monroe County**, enlisted in 1861 and wounded and captured in Virginia in 1862. The soldier is a promising candidate for C042, but neither his identity as the Châteauguay-born Paul nor his proposed 1844 marriage to Monique Champagne is proved. New parish readings also extend the **Marie Gendron–Étienne Boursier** household through January 1839.

The earlier original censuses establish a Benjamin/Victoria household in Michigan in 1850, 1860 and 1870; the original 1834 Québec marriage establishes Benjamin Gendron as Catherine’s guardian. Joining those two Benjamins still requires a record naming family or former spouse. The Sherrington Louis remains within a competing Pauline/Apolline Boyer family context, so Sherrington is not an established later destination for Toussaint Louis Gendron, father of Pierre. The later sections give exact sources and distinguish identified households from unproved cross-record identities.

Pierre Gendron and Marie Madeleine Paré themselves still have no secure later destination from this work. C042 Paul and C181 Étienne remain **Needs work**. C080 Jean Baptiste and C111 Charles remain **Needs work**; C240 Toussaint retains his existing **possible elimination** assessment, with the claimed infant burial unverified.

## Continuation: Catherine’s guardian and another Québec child

The original handwritten **14 July 1834** marriage of **Gratien Martin and Catherine Gendron** was inspected in the two images published by the Société patrimoine et histoire de l’Île-Bizard et Sainte-Geneviève. Catherine is a minor daughter of **deceased Joseph Gendron and deceased Marguerite Daigneault**, domiciled in the parish. The witnesses include **Benjamin Gendron, “tuteur de dite épouse”**—the bride’s guardian. Gratien is a minor son of deceased Joseph Martin and Josephte Legault dite Deslauriers. ### Estate-index and guardianship searches

The official [BAnQ Montréal-area post-mortem inventory index, 1791–1840](https://www2.banq.qc.ca/archives/genealogie_histoire_familiale/ressources/bd/recherche.html?id=INVENTAIRE_MONTREAL_20170823), was searched with all unused form fields explicitly blank. The male-surname searches returned **23 Gendron** and **4 Gendreau** entries; the female-surname search returned **37 Daigneau** entries. None matched Joseph Gendron and Marguerite Daigneau. Female Daigneault, Daignault and Deneau each returned zero. Truncated search terms Gendr and Daign also returned zero, demonstrating that those attempts did not provide an effective broader spelling search. An initial malformed query populated all fields and was discarded, not counted as a negative.

These are index negatives within stated coverage, not proof that no inventory or guardianship existed. The database itself warns that spellings were not standardized. The public [BAnQ tutelle/curatelle database](https://www2.banq.qc.ca/archives/genealogie_histoire_familiale/ressources/bd/recherche.html?id=TUTELLE_CURATELLE_20170823) covers **1791–1807**, so it cannot answer the 1830–1834 question. Targeted public catalogue searches did not locate the specific appointment; they were not a complete court-index search.

The third pass checked additional court-index coverage. [BAnQ Thémis 1](https://www2.banq.qc.ca/archives/genealogie_histoire_familiale/ressources/bd/recherche.html?id=THEMIS_1) describes Montréal higher civil cases for **1794–1827**, ending before Joseph’s death and Catherine’s documented guardianship. Its later **1828–1850** block and hearing registers were not searched. The [UQAM CHRS dataset list](https://chrs.uqam.ca/index.php/liste-des-jeux-de-donnees/) describes another set beginning in **1840**; its heading and description differ on the ending year, but neither covers the early 1830s. Exact-name searches for Benjamin’s tutelle and the parents’ inventory produced no identifiable file. These coverage checks do not constitute a negative search of the relevant guardianship records.

## Benjamin: possible move to Monroe County, Michigan

An [Ancestry compiled profile](https://www.ancestry.com.au/genealogy/records/benjamin-jandreau-jondro-24-22rsc8b) attaches Joseph Gendron and Marguerite Daigneau to Benjamin Jandreau/Jondro, reportedly born in Châteauguay about 1809 and deceased at Monroe on 13 March 1871. It lists children born before and after 1839. This is a tree assertion, not a contemporary parentage record.

[WikiTree Gendron-899](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Gendron-899), read through the public API, gives a marriage to Victoria Marie Guillot on **29 April 1839 at St-Antoine, River Raisin**. Its biography cites Denissen, *Genealogy of the French Families of the Detroit River Region*, revised 1987, **page 516, family 1**, and four census records. It attaches no parents to Benjamin. The spouse field calls the place Michigan Territory, an anachronistic label for the stated date; that wording is not adopted as historical geography.

The [official GENDIS entry for Benjamine Johndron](https://vitalstats.michigan.gov/osr/gendisx/scripts/individual.asp?UniqueID=90909), read directly on the state website, gives:

- Death **11 March 1871**, **Frenchtown, Monroe County**.
- Married male farmer, **72 years old**, birthplace **Europe**.
- Both parents unknown.
- Ledger **page 82, record 788**, recorded **21 June 1872**.

The index's age and birthplace conflict with the compiled Canadian birth about 1809, and the death dates differ by two days. The ledger image has not been read. These discrepancies cannot simply be called indexing errors.

The verified Québec Benjamin had a wife named Marguerite Bourbonnais and daughters baptized in October 1831 and September 1834. No death of that wife or widower description has been found to bridge him to the alleged 1839 Michigan marriage. A targeted check of [Marguerite's NosOrigines profile](https://www.nosorigines.qc.ca/GenealogieQuebec.aspx?genealogie=Bourbonnais_Marguerite&pid=1289540) and exact couple searches produced no later event. Its numerical marriage ages are not present in the inspected archival transcription.

The Ancestry child list also supplies Margaret Jondro (1831–1906), Roselle Gendron (born 1834), and Benjamin Gendron (1837–1914). Exact and variant searches for Margaret and the younger Benjamin found no mother-naming record. Margaret's birth year is compatible with the verified October 1831 baptism, but identity remains unproved. The official Michiganology search endpoint was inaccessible; its records were not searched. The subsequent 1850 census reading, below, verifies the household’s Roselle and younger Benjamin without establishing their mother.

## Paul: a new Monroe-to-Virginia military lead

Grosvenor’s Report”** names **“Paul Jondro, of Monroe”** among Company D’s wounded during the reported six days of fighting. ### Federal index and the unresolved family link

The [National Park Service soldier detail](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/civilwar/search-soldiers-detail.htm?soldierId=B0112BAD-DC7A-DF11-BF36-B8AC6F5D926A) confirms **Paul Jondrieux, Company D, 7th Michigan Infantry**, rank in and out **Private**, with the exact index locator **M545, roll 21**. Its public search also returns **Jandrieux, Johndrieux and Jumdreux**, each cross-referenced to Paul Jondrieux in the same regiment. These are variant index entries, not four separate soldiers.

The evidence now gives a coherent **Monroe County, 1861 → Virginia, 1862** trail for this soldier. A Paul baptized in May 1818 would have been about **43** at enlistment, compared with the book’s **44**: close, but not exact. The location fits the reported **6 May 1844 Paul Gendron–Monique/Monica Huyet dit Champagne marriage at Monroe**, which remains unread in the original register. None of the inspected military or newspaper entries names the soldier’s wife, parents or birthplace. Accordingly, **the soldier is not yet identified as either the 1844 groom or C042**.

The next discriminating records are his **original descriptive/enlistment record, compiled military service file, prisoner/parole or hospital records, and any pension application by him, a widow or minor children**. The surname variants and **military service index M545, roll 21**, give precise starting points; that locator is not a pension-file reference. No original personal service or pension file was obtained in this pass.

A completed independent check covered the entire **Monroe County section of the federal *List of Pensioners on the Roll January 1, 1883*, volume IV, printed pages 345–348**: [345](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1796458/m1/349/), [346](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1796458/m1/350/), [347](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1796458/m1/351/), [348](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1796458/m1/352/). No identifiable Paul/Monica or target surname variant appeared. This tests pensioners listed with a Monroe County post-office address at that date only. It does not establish that no application existed, or that no pension was paid at another time or place. The general and organization pension indexes were **not directly searched**; public Fold3 access failed, and exact-name web discovery searches produced no personal pension, prisoner or hospital file.

## Sherrington Louis: a substantial competing-family context

The original **1851 census, pages 45–46**, was visually inspected. Page 45, line 10, records Louis Goudron/Goudrau, a widowed labourer aged 98, whose birthplace reads Verchères. Lines 5–9 contain Polin Boyer, widow, 50, born La Prairie, followed by Denise/Dinas, 20; Joseph, 12; Euphima, 11; and Célina, 7. Page 46 places lines 1–10 in one one-story log house with two families; the next house starts on line 11. Louis is marked as a resident member. Thus he shares a dwelling with the Knight and Boyer groups. The census states no father–daughter relationship.

## Toussaint Gendron and Claire Rollin: border-area leads

[Claire's NosOrigines profile](https://www.nosorigines.qc.ca/GenealogieQuebec.aspx?genealogie=Rollin_Claire&pid=1281586) reports a March 1865 death at **“Dundee, USA”** and burial **3 May 1865 at Beauharnois**. The original burial must resolve the place and any circumstances of return. The country label has not been silently changed. Her remarriage to Jean-Baptiste Gendron on 2 June 1840 remains part of the established research context.

For their daughter Zoé, wife of Louis Ranger, a [NYGenWeb newspaper transcription](https://franklin.nygenweb.net/bios/barney.html), section 2.3, supplies a possible collateral route. The *Malone Farmer*, 23 August 1933, reports that Carrie Ranger Barney, daughter of Louis Ranger of Beauharnois, moved with her parents to Fort Covington, later lived in Boston, and died there on 15 August 1933. A sister, Mrs. Edward Gendron, lived in Montréal. **The mother's name is absent, so this cannot yet be assigned to Zoé's family.** The original newspaper was not inspected.

The useful next discriminator is Carrie's Massachusetts death record dated 15 August 1933, or her marriage to Joseph Barney/Bernier before their reported son's birth in 1875. The public Fort Covington marriage and vital-record compilations yielded no mother-naming connection.

LAC lists the **1851 Dundee census as missing**. The [official district guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/genealogy-family-history/censuses/pre-confederation/1851/districts/canada-east-quebec.html) therefore explains an unavailable search, not evidence of the family's absence. The **1861 Dundee/St-Régis** census, reel pointer **C-1282**, was located and its name pages screened in the third pass, as described below.
