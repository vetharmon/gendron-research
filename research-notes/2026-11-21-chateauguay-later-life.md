# Châteauguay families: later-life tracing — updated 22 November 2026

## Assessment

The fourth pass strengthens the **Joseph Gendron–Marguerite Daigneau family** with Marie’s **25 November 1828 marriage**, read in the archival typed copy. It names **Benjamin and Louis Gendron explicitly as her brothers**. Marie’s identity is now anchored to the parents; Louis becomes a separate sibling route whose later life remains unknown. Catherine’s original **1851 Saint-Timothée census household** remains the clearest established later residence for a sister. The proposed later deaths of Catherine and Marie still need their parish records.

Two new original American records supply useful later-life evidence. The **1880 Fort Covington census** contains a **Louis Ranger, 51, shoemaker**, with wife **Mary** and children **Emma, Marsha and John**. His age and trade fit the compiled husband of Zoé Gendron, but neither **Mary = Zoé** nor **Marsha = Marcelline, wife of Edward Gendron**, is proved. The **1932 obituary of Celestine Jondro Fix** traces her from **Berlin Township to Monroe and burial at Oldport**. Her age is compatible with the Celestine in Benjamin and Victoria’s earlier household, but the obituary does not name her parents.

The Michigan Benjamin/Victoria household is established in original censuses for **1850, 1860 and 1870**. Connecting that man to the Québec Benjamin—Marie’s brother, Catherine’s guardian and Marguerite Bourbonnais’s husband—still requires a linking record. The contemporary newspaper and official printed service entry establish a **Paul Jondro/Jondrieux of Monroe → Virginia, 1861–1862** trail, but do not identify him as C042 or establish his fate after capture. All **12 original scans in one Seventh Michigan Infantry discharge folder** were checked in this pass without finding Paul. More precise descriptive-roll and index routes are now documented below; his personal military and pension files remain unread.

Pierre Gendron and Marie Madeleine Paré still have no secure later destination. The Sherrington Louis remains within a competing Pauline/Apolline Boyer family context. **C042 Paul, C181 Étienne, C080 Jean Baptiste and C111 Charles remain Needs work; C240 Toussaint retains his existing possible elimination assessment**, with the claimed infant burial unverified. The new collateral evidence does not justify changing those classifications.

## Continuation: Catherine’s guardian and another Québec child

The original handwritten **14 July 1834** marriage of **Gratien Martin and Catherine Gendron** was inspected in the two images published by the Société patrimoine et histoire de l’Île-Bizard et Sainte-Geneviève. Catherine is a minor daughter of **deceased Joseph Gendron and deceased Marguerite Daigneault**, domiciled in the parish. The witnesses include **Benjamin Gendron, “tuteur de dite épouse”**—the bride’s guardian. Gratien is a minor son of deceased Joseph Martin and Josephte Legault dite Deslauriers. ### Estate-index and guardianship searches

The official [BAnQ Montréal-area post-mortem inventory index, 1791–1840](https://www2.banq.qc.ca/archives/genealogie_histoire_familiale/ressources/bd/recherche.html?id=INVENTAIRE_MONTREAL_20170823), was searched with all unused form fields explicitly blank. The male-surname searches returned **23 Gendron** and **4 Gendreau** entries; the female-surname search returned **37 Daigneau** entries. None matched Joseph Gendron and Marguerite Daigneau. Female Daigneault, Daignault and Deneau each returned zero. Truncated search terms Gendr and Daign also returned zero, demonstrating that those attempts did not provide an effective broader spelling search. An initial malformed query populated all fields and was discarded, not counted as a negative.

These are index negatives within stated coverage, not proof that no inventory or guardianship existed. The database itself warns that spellings were not standardized. The public [BAnQ tutelle/curatelle database](https://www2.banq.qc.ca/archives/genealogie_histoire_familiale/ressources/bd/recherche.html?id=TUTELLE_CURATELLE_20170823) covers **1791–1807**, so it cannot answer the 1830–1834 question. Targeted public catalogue searches did not locate the specific appointment; they were not a complete court-index search.

The third pass checked additional court-index coverage. [BAnQ Thémis 1](https://www2.banq.qc.ca/archives/genealogie_histoire_familiale/ressources/bd/recherche.html?id=THEMIS_1) describes Montréal higher civil cases for **1794–1827**, ending before Joseph’s death and Catherine’s documented guardianship. Its later **1828–1850** block and hearing registers were not searched. The [UQAM CHRS dataset list](https://chrs.uqam.ca/index.php/liste-des-jeux-de-donnees/) describes another set beginning in **1840**; its heading and description differ on the ending year, but neither covers the early 1830s. Exact-name searches for Benjamin’s tutelle and the parents’ inventory produced no identifiable file. These coverage checks do not constitute a negative search of the relevant guardianship records.

### Original census: Catherine and Gatien at Saint-Timothée

The third pass located and visually inspected the **1851 Canada East census, Saint-Timothée, Beauharnois**, printed pages **83–84**, lines **24–33**. The names page records **Gatien Martin, 40, cultivateur**, **Catherine Gendron, 36**, and eight younger Martins: **Joseph 17, Julie 16, Emilie 13, Pierre 11, Marie 9, Cadie 7, Moïse 4 and Léonidas 2**. Joseph is also a cultivateur. The reverse identifies the place as Saint-Timothée, comprising an island and five concessions, and groups these lines in one wooden house occupied by one family.

The couple’s names and the children’s sequence support identification with the family formed in the verified 1834 marriage. ## Benjamin: possible move to Monroe County, Michigan

An [Ancestry compiled profile](https://www.ancestry.com.au/genealogy/records/benjamin-jandreau-jondro-24-22rsc8b) attaches Joseph Gendron and Marguerite Daigneau to Benjamin Jandreau/Jondro, reportedly born in Châteauguay about 1809 and deceased at Monroe on 13 March 1871. It lists children born before and after 1839. This is a tree assertion, not a contemporary parentage record.

[WikiTree Gendron-899](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Gendron-899), read through the public API, gives a marriage to Victoria Marie Guillot on **29 April 1839 at St-Antoine, River Raisin**. Its biography cites Denissen, *Genealogy of the French Families of the Detroit River Region*, revised 1987, **page 516, family 1**, and four census records. It attaches no parents to Benjamin. The spouse field calls the place Michigan Territory, an anachronistic label for the stated date; that wording is not adopted as historical geography.

The [official GENDIS entry for Benjamine Johndron](https://vitalstats.michigan.gov/osr/gendisx/scripts/individual.asp?UniqueID=90909), read directly on the state website, gives:

- Death **11 March 1871**, **Frenchtown, Monroe County**.
- Married male farmer, **72 years old**, birthplace **Europe**.
- Both parents unknown.
- Ledger **page 82, record 788**, recorded **21 June 1872**.

The index's age and birthplace conflict with the compiled Canadian birth about 1809, and the death dates differ by two days. The ledger image has not been read. These discrepancies cannot simply be called indexing errors.

The verified Québec Benjamin had a wife named Marguerite Bourbonnais and daughters baptized in October 1831 and September 1834. No death of that wife or widower description has been found to bridge him to the alleged 1839 Michigan marriage. A targeted check of [Marguerite's NosOrigines profile](https://www.nosorigines.qc.ca/GenealogieQuebec.aspx?genealogie=Bourbonnais_Marguerite&pid=1289540) and exact couple searches produced no later event. Its numerical marriage ages are not present in the inspected archival transcription.

The Ancestry child list also supplies Margaret Jondro (1831–1906), Roselle Gendron (born 1834), and Benjamin Gendron (1837–1914). Exact and variant searches for Margaret and the younger Benjamin found no mother-naming record. Margaret's birth year is compatible with the verified October 1831 baptism, but identity remains unproved. The Michiganology civil-record search endpoint was inaccessible; no relevant civil death record was searched through it. The separate fourth-pass Preservica military search is described below. The subsequent 1850 census reading, below, verifies the household’s Roselle and younger Benjamin without establishing their mother.

### Federal index and the unresolved family link

The [National Park Service soldier detail](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/civilwar/search-soldiers-detail.htm?soldierId=B0112BAD-DC7A-DF11-BF36-B8AC6F5D926A) confirms **Paul Jondrieux, Company D, 7th Michigan Infantry**, rank in and out **Private**, with the exact index locator **M545, roll 21**. The evidence now gives a coherent **Monroe County, 1861 → Virginia, 1862** trail for this soldier. None of the inspected military or newspaper entries names the soldier’s wife, parents or birthplace. Accordingly, **the soldier is not yet identified as either the 1844 groom or C042**.
