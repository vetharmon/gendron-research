# Châteauguay families: later-life tracing — 21 November 2026

## Assessment

No candidate assessment changes in this pass. The strongest new migration lead is Benjamin Gendron of the Joseph Gendron–Marguerite Daigneau family, possibly the Benjamin Jondro/Jondron later found in Monroe County, Michigan. That identity is not established. The strongest correction is a competing family context for the elderly Louis recorded at Sherrington: his census dwelling contains the Pauline/Apolline Boyer family. Sherrington is therefore not an established later destination for Toussaint Louis Gendron, father of Pierre.

Pierre Gendron and Marie Madeleine Paré themselves still have no secure later destination from this work. C042 Paul and C181 Étienne remain **Needs work**. C080 Jean Baptiste and C111 Charles remain **Needs work**; C240 Toussaint retains his existing **possible elimination** assessment, with the claimed infant burial unverified.

## Benjamin: possible move to Monroe County, Michigan

An [Ancestry compiled profile](https://www.ancestry.com.au/genealogy/records/benjamin-jandreau-jondro-24-22rsc8b) attaches Joseph Gendron and Marguerite Daigneau to Benjamin Jandreau/Jondro, reportedly born in Châteauguay about 1809 and deceased at Monroe on 13 March 1871. It lists children born before and after 1839. This is a tree assertion, not a contemporary parentage record.

[WikiTree Gendron-899](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Gendron-899), read through the public API, gives a marriage to Victoria Marie Guillot on **29 April 1839 at St-Antoine, River Raisin**. Its biography cites Denissen, *Genealogy of the French Families of the Detroit River Region*, revised 1987, **page 516, family 1**, and four census records. It attaches no parents to Benjamin. The spouse field calls the place Michigan Territory, an anachronistic label for the stated date; that wording is not adopted as historical geography.

The [official GENDIS entry for Benjamine Johndron](https://vitalstats.michigan.gov/osr/gendisx/scripts/individual.asp?UniqueID=90909), read directly on the state website, gives:

- Death **11 March 1871**, **Frenchtown, Monroe County**.
- Married male farmer, **72 years old**, birthplace **Europe**.
- Both parents unknown.
- Ledger **page 82, record 788**, recorded **21 June 1872**.

The index's age and birthplace conflict with the compiled Canadian birth about 1809, and the death dates differ by two days. The ledger image has not been read. These discrepancies cannot simply be called indexing errors.

The verified Québec Benjamin had a wife named Marguerite Bourbonnais and a daughter baptized in October 1831. No death of that wife or widower description has been found to bridge him to the alleged 1839 Michigan marriage. A targeted check of [Marguerite's NosOrigines profile](https://www.nosorigines.qc.ca/GenealogieQuebec.aspx?genealogie=Bourbonnais_Marguerite&pid=1289540) and exact couple searches produced no later event. Its numerical marriage ages are not present in the inspected archival transcription.

The Ancestry child list also supplies Margaret Jondro (1831–1906), Roselle Gendron (born 1834), and Benjamin Gendron (1837–1914). Exact and variant searches for Margaret and the younger Benjamin found no mother-naming record. Margaret's birth year is compatible with the verified October 1831 baptism, but identity remains unproved. The official Michiganology search endpoint was inaccessible; its records were not searched.

## Sherrington Louis: a substantial competing-family context

The original **1851 census, pages 45–46**, was visually inspected. Page 45, line 10, records Louis Goudron/Goudrau, a widowed labourer aged 98, whose birthplace reads Verchères. Lines 5–9 contain Polin Boyer, widow, 50, born La Prairie, followed by Denise/Dinas, 20; Joseph, 12; Euphima, 11; and Célina, 7. Page 46 places lines 1–10 in one one-story log house with two families; the next house starts on line 11. Louis is marked as a resident member. Thus he shares a dwelling with the Knight and Boyer groups. The census states no father–daughter relationship.

- [1851 names and birthplaces, e002303223](https://central.bac-lac.gc.ca/.item/?id=e002303223&app=census1851&op=img)
- [1851 house and residence columns, e002303224](https://central.bac-lac.gc.ca/.item/?id=e002303224&app=census1851&op=img)

The original **1861 census, Saint-Patrick-de-Sherrington, page 287, lines 45–48**, independently records **Appoline Gendron, 59, widow**, with **Denise Boyer, 28**, **Joseph Boyer, 24**, and **Célina Boyer, 17**. Denise is marked as an absent family member. The others are resident family members. The repeated names support identifying the earlier Polin Boyer group as Apolline Gendron's family; the age differences are retained.

[1861 image 4108803_00610](https://central.bac-lac.gc.ca/.item/?id=4108803_00610&app=census1861&op=img)

SGQ's *L'Ancêtre*, vol. 36, no. 288, autumn 2009, p. 63, answer 6051, assigns the same 1851 Louis and 1853 burial to **Louis Bonaventure Gendron, husband of Geneviève Raymond**. This published identification is a competing claim, not a resolved correction. [Issue PDF](https://www.sgq.qc.ca/client_file/upload/L-Ancetre/Les-premieres-annees/V36-N288.pdf)

Public WikiTree biographies connect Apolline to Louis and Geneviève and give original-image links, but those parish images were not inspected here. [Louis Gendron-1309](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Gendron-1309) explicitly leaves his death unresolved; [Joseph Boyer-8445](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Boyer-8445) supplies the census trail. A 1775 birth conflicts substantially with the reported ages 98 and 95. The compilations also disagree on the July baptism day and the mother's surname. These discrepancies remain open.

**Result:** the 1851 dwelling and 1861 family comparison materially weaken use of the Sherrington man as evidence for Pierre's father, Toussaint Louis. No replacement identification of the 1853 burial is proved. Pierre and Marie Madeleine Paré cannot be placed at Sherrington on this evidence.

## Toussaint Gendron and Claire Rollin: border-area leads

[Claire's NosOrigines profile](https://www.nosorigines.qc.ca/GenealogieQuebec.aspx?genealogie=Rollin_Claire&pid=1281586) reports a March 1865 death at **“Dundee, USA”** and burial **3 May 1865 at Beauharnois**. The original burial must resolve the place and any circumstances of return. The country label has not been silently changed. Her remarriage to Jean-Baptiste Gendron on 2 June 1840 remains part of the established research context.

For their daughter Zoé, wife of Louis Ranger, a [NYGenWeb newspaper transcription](https://franklin.nygenweb.net/bios/barney.html), section 2.3, supplies a possible collateral route. The *Malone Farmer*, 23 August 1933, reports that Carrie Ranger Barney, daughter of Louis Ranger of Beauharnois, moved with her parents to Fort Covington, later lived in Boston, and died there on 15 August 1933. A sister, Mrs. Edward Gendron, lived in Montréal. **The mother's name is absent, so this cannot yet be assigned to Zoé's family.** The original newspaper was not inspected.

The useful next discriminator is Carrie's Massachusetts death record dated 15 August 1933, or her marriage to Joseph Barney/Bernier before their reported son's birth in 1875. The public Fort Covington marriage and vital-record compilations yielded no mother-naming connection.

LAC lists the **1851 Dundee census as missing**. The [official district guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/library-archives/collection/research-help/genealogy-family-history/censuses/pre-confederation/1851/districts/canada-east-quebec.html) therefore explains an unavailable search, not evidence of the family's absence. The 1861 Dundee/St-Regis reel pointer is C-1282; that census was not swept in this pass.

- **Paul, GENDIS:** Monroe County, first name Paul, all available 1867–1897, produced sixteen summaries without an identifiable Gendron/Jondro variant. The county filter is incomplete for much of the 1890s; this is not a statewide death negative.
- **GENDIS surname *ndr*, Monroe:** eighteen summaries included Benjamine Johndron and a younger Oliver Jondron. Oliver died 5 November 1876, Monroe Town, age 28 years 10 months, born Michigan, parents Oliver and Ellen Jondron; [entry 208616](https://vitalstats.michigan.gov/osr/gendisx/scripts/individual.asp?UniqueID=208616). He is too young for C110. His father remains an unassigned name lead.
- **Monroe Commercial 1871:** the [two-page obituary index](https://mymcls.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Commercial-Obituaries-1871.pdf) yielded no matching Benjamin/Gendron/Jondro entry. This covers that index only.
- **Isaac Foucher/Faucher:** SGQ questions 6064–6065 in the 2009 issue concern the Ottawa Isaac–Louise Barbe family. No question-number match was found in public issues 289–299. This neither proves that no later answer exists nor connects that couple to Châteauguay's Isaac and Charlotte Gendron.
- **Retrieval limits:** the underlying Michigan census ARKs, original 1839/1844 marriages, and death ledger were not read. Dibean's two public Monroe marriage-file hosts returned 406/503, so no absence is inferred from them. The findings above distinguish original census images, archival typed copies, state index entries and compiled family claims.
