## How to improve the research without inflating the results

**Separate identity from elimination.** First establish which adult or deceased child belongs to the baptism. Then explain how that life excludes Franklin. An identified 1836 or 1842 marriage is a valuable research advance, but does not by itself exclude a later Missouri household. Conversely, a continuing family can establish separation without locating a burial.

**Allow a cumulative identity argument.** Do not require one document to state every parent, sibling and alias. C095's existing solid conclusion illustrates the method: compatible age, same parish, both parents, independently documented maternal-name variation and an associate in a brother's burial reinforce each other. For C247, the right next step is more sibling evidence, not waiting exclusively for a parent-naming death record.

**Apply age tolerance consistently.** Keep the reported age and the calculated age side by side. Treat an index's inferred birth year differently from an original birth date. Baptism can occur months after birth. A two- or three-year mismatch usually warrants family identification, not automatic rejection. Conflicting parents remain material and require investigation; age tolerance does not make them disappear.

**Work related candidates together.** The useful clusters include C010/C212; C034/C195; C039/C065; C042/C181; C080/C111/C240; C143/C178; and C237/C266/C152. Propagate a verified family fact to each relevant page, while preserving uncertainty about an individual son.

**Make negative searches reproducible.** A completed check should state collection, parish, year coverage, name variants, index versus original, page or image range where relevant, and access gaps. “Not found” means not found in that search. An agricultural schedule is a potential land clue; a blank compiled child list is not proof that a couple had no children. Repeated copied trees are one derivative claim, not multiple independent sources.

**Keep next actions distinct from historical logs.** Record the result once, keep it as history, and replace the current next action when it has been completed. The priority sheet has been refreshed for this reason. C010/C106's different grades remain an explicit grading-review item; no count was improved merely by relabelling a candidate.

Ancestry access would be most useful for the exact records and family comparisons above. More broad same-name tree searches, or repeated attempts at already restricted images, are less likely to resolve the remaining questions.
