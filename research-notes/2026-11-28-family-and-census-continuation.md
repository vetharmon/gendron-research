# Family relationships and original census readings — 28 November 2026

This continuation adds selected record texts, resolves a census locality error, and gives several Needs work candidates more specific family targets. **The 26 Needs work assessments remain unchanged.** A stated relationship is preserved where the source gives one; a shared surname, neighbouring household or sponsor pairing is not treated as an identification.

## C080: the baptism names a godfather described as brother

The later archival typed Saint-Clément copy records **Lucie**, born **18 December 1842** and baptized **19 December**, daughter of **Jean Baptiste Gendron and Claire Rollin**. Her godfather is **Jean Baptiste Gendron, frère**; her godmother is **Lucie Gendron**, without a stated relationship. Two readers independently checked the complete entry. [Typed copy, volume 3, p. 1119, image 60](https://heritage.canadiana.ca/view/oocihm.lac_reel_c2890/60)

This supplies a precise relationship to test in the maternal stepfamily already identified in the research log through Claire’s 1840 remarriage. The godfather’s own parents are not named, and the copy does not distinguish a full brother from a half brother. Reconstructing both spouses’ earlier children and comparing the contemporary handwritten baptism are necessary before identifying him as C080, the son of Toussaint Gendron and Claire Rolin.

A separate **13 June 1842** entry names a Lucie born that day to **Louis Roy and Claire Rollin**, with **Joseph Gendron and Lucie Gendron** as sponsors. Its different father and date are retained. These entries are not merged, and the identity of the two women named Claire Rollin is not established from the copies. [June entry, volume 3, p. 1077, image 18](https://heritage.canadiana.ca/view/oocihm.lac_reel_c2890/18)

Complete French entries and English translations are now attached to [C080](https://vetharmon.github.io/gendron-research/candidates/c080/). They are explicitly labelled later typed copies, not newly read contemporary manuscripts.

## C264: the Ontario household’s more specific birthplace

The original **1861 Kennebec personal census**, Frontenac district 39, printed page 9 / handwritten page 8, lines 22–24, records **Joseph Shandraw, 28**, farmer; **Mary A. Shandraw, 17**, born Sheffield; and **Battysh Shandraw, 24**, labourer. Ages are at next birthday. Joseph and Battysh have a birthplace written **Shatagee L C**, with Joseph’s first vowel less distinct. **Châteauguay, Lower Canada is the interpretation**, not a replacement for the spelling. All three are Roman Catholic. [Original nominal page](https://data2.collectionscanada.gc.ca/1861/jpg/4391548_00053.jpg)

Joseph and Mary A. are marked married and family members. Battysh is explicitly a resident **not a member of the family**. The census supplies no parents or explicit relationship between the men. The classification does not prove that they were unrelated, and the unusual given name is not silently changed to Baptiste or John B.

The combination of names, Sheffield birthplace and Kennebec residence strongly correlates the couple with the Ontario Shandrow–Clark family. Joseph’s age fits an approximately 1833 birth better than C264’s 1829 baptism; Battysh’s age also differs from C264’s known brother’s 1832 baptism. These differences require an identifying record rather than an automatic match or exclusion.

The original enumerator’s map places **Joseph Shandraw in the eastern portion of lot 14, concession V**, with a small caption read **E½**. John Forsythe’s certification is dated **16 February 1861**. The map supplies the given name independently of the agricultural entry whose given name is clipped; it does not alter that transcription or settle later lot-portion conflicts. [Original map](https://data2.collectionscanada.gc.ca/1861/jpg/4391548_00040.jpg)

The official Ontario catalogue introduces an unresolved retrieval discrepancy: it assigns **RG 1-386 volume 2 to 1845–1856**, with all volumes on **MS 693 reel 190**. The previously inspected crosswalk assigns volume 2, pages 397–end, to **reel 191**, while the index refers the **1866** sale to volume 2, page 446. The actual volume cover and sale remain unread. [Official catalogue](https://aims.archives.gov.on.ca/scripts/mwimain.dll/144/DESCRIPTION_WEB/WEB_DESC_DET?SESSIONSEARCH&exp=sisn%207408) · [C264’s source comparison](https://vetharmon.github.io/gendron-research/candidates/c264/)

## C266: the actual Sainte-Brigide household

The original **1871 census** confirms the locality as **Sainte-Brigide**, Iberville district 124, division 2, page 51. Family **197** contains **Joseph Gendron, 40**, a widowed day labourer, **Césarie, 7**, and **Odéna, 4**, both female. The immediately preceding **separate family 196** contains **Joseph Gendron, 65**, a married farmer, and **Marie, 64**, married. [Original page](https://data2.collectionscanada.gc.ca/1871/jpg/4395448_00122.jpg)

There is no relationship column. The girls are enumerated with the younger Joseph; they are not proven daughters from this record alone. The older couple is not proven to be his parents. A parent-naming record for **Césarie, around 1863–64**, or **Odéna, around 1866–67**, or Joseph’s widowhood/remarriage record is needed to test the qualified C275 comparison against C266’s François Gendron / Marianne–Marie Anne Élisabeth Dupont parents. [C266’s selected French/English source and assessment](https://vetharmon.github.io/gendron-research/candidates/c266/)
