IDENTITY RESEARCH · QUÉBEC 1816–1830

Who became Franklin Jondro?

This project investigates whether François Jandron, baptized at Châteauguay on 6 September 1823, became Franklin Jondro, the Canadian-born farmer who settled in Missouri and died there in 1886. It brings together Franklin’s story and the records used to compare him with the other Gendron and Gendreau candidates.

From Châteauguay to Missouri

François was baptized on 6 September 1823 at Saint-Joachim-de-Châteauguay, in the Montréal orbit of Lower Canada. The register spells the family name as it was heard — Jandron. His father, Pierre Jandron, was a day labourer; his mother was Marie Paré, who had married Pierre at Caughnawaga in 1818. François was the youngest of the three children recorded to the couple. No later Québec marriage or burial has been securely identified for him.

Franklin’s American records place him in St. Clair County, Missouri, in 1850 with his wife Phebe A., born in Kentucky, and their infant son Isaac. In the 1860 census of Polk Township he appears with Polly A., identified in the family evidence as Mary Ann Lord, and their children, including Nancy Ann. That census gives his birthplace as “Montreal Canada.” By 1880 he was in Dallas Township, Missouri, with Mary and their family.

Franklin Jondro died on 13 April 1886. His gravestone gives his birth as 22 July 1820; the lifetime census and draft ages imply birth years of approximately 1822–1826. These differences are part of the identity question. The research compares his American evidence with the Châteauguay family and follows the other candidates through their own records.
