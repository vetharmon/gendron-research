IDENTITY RESEARCH · QUÉBEC 1816–1830

Who became Franklin Jondro?

François Jandron, baptized at Châteauguay on 6 September 1823, crossed into the United States, settled in Missouri, and died there in 1886 as Franklin Jondro. This site tells his story — and keeps the evidence, candidate by candidate, that separates him from every other man who might have been him.

From Châteauguay to Missouri

François was baptized on 6 September 1823 at Saint-Joachim-de-Châteauguay, in the Montréal orbit of Lower Canada. The register spells the family name as it was heard — Jandron. His father, Pierre Jandron, was a day labourer; his mother was Marie Paré, who had married Pierre at Caughnawaga in 1818. François was the youngest of the three children recorded to the couple, and after his baptism the family leaves no further trace in the Québec registers: no marriage, no burial, no children of his own baptized there.

He next appears an ocean of farmland away. In the 1850 census of St. Clair County, Missouri, Franklin Jondro is living with his wife Phoebe — born Phoebe Phillips, of Kentucky — and their infant son Isaac. Phoebe died about 1850, and in 1852 Franklin remarried, to Mary Polly Ann Lord, also in St. Clair County. The 1860 census, in Polk Township, gives his birthplace as “Montreal Canada” — the closest he ever came to naming Châteauguay himself. By 1880 he was in Dallas Township, Missouri, with Mary and their children, among them a daughter, Nancy Ann, born in 1856.

Franklin Jondro died on 13 April 1886. His gravestone gives his birth as 22 July 1820 — a date no record supports. His ages across the American censuses point anywhere from 1819 to 1830, which is exactly the problem this site exists to solve: a man whose own records never quite agree about him has to be identified by eliminating everyone else.

The proof at a glance

The register behind this page checks every Gendron and Gendreau baptism recorded from 1816 to 1830 in the parishes Franklin could plausibly have come from — {{ counted_candidates | size }} distinct candidates, each followed into the marriage, burial and census records. {{ n_elim }} are eliminated on primary records; {{ n_open }} remain unresolved; one, François of Châteauguay, is the proposed identity. The counts below are computed from the register itself, so they cannot drift from the candidate pages.

One man cannot be eliminated, and he is named here rather than swept aside: François Xavier Gendron, baptized at Châteauguay in 1815, who leaves no later record in Québec. The identification of Franklin rests on his own positive record and on the elimination of everyone else — with that single exception standing in the open.
