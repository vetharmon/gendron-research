## Biography

Joseph Gendron was born on 16 February 1825 and baptized the following day at Sainte-Anne-de-la-Pérade, son of François Gendron, cultivateur, and Marie-Josephte Tessier. He is eliminated through an identified adult marriage at Saint-Casimir on 3 September 1861 to Betsy Connely (Élisabeth Connolly), daughter of Michael Connely and Mary Hamilton.

The identification uses three original acts. Joseph’s marriage names his father François Gendron of Sainte-Anne and explicitly calls Olivier Leduc and Hilaire Dusablon the groom’s brothers-in-law. Olivier’s own marriage, celebrated on 15 February 1847 at Sainte-Anne-de-la-Pérade, names his bride Phébée Gendron as the daughter of François Gendron and Josephte Tessier. Olivier therefore supplies the family connection between the adult groom and the parents named in Joseph’s baptism. This is a connected Québec identity in 1861, incompatible with Franklin Jondro’s established Missouri marriage and family.

**Record limitation retained:** the FamilySearch copy of Joseph’s 1861 marriage omits his mother. The conclusion does not silently supply her name to that act: her identification comes from the 1825 baptism and the 1847 marriage of the groom’s sister. NosOrigines reports that the other register copy names the mother, but that copy was not inspected and is not needed for this conclusion. Hilaire Dusablon’s relationship is visible in the 1861 act; his own marriage was not independently checked in this pass.
