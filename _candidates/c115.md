## Research assessment

Baptism/index act: 2870966
CURRENT STATUS: Eliminated — original images verified
Conclusion: The 1823 child of Abraham Gendreau and Marie Pellerin is correlated with the unmarried Édouard Gendreau, carpenter, age37, in the same parental family at St-Thomas, Montmagny, in the 1861 census. This Canadian unmarried identity conflicts with Franklin Jondro’s established wife and children in Missouri in1860. The correlation uses original baptism and original census household evidence, independently corroborated by Herménégilde’s baptism. This is a reasoned combined-evidence exclusion; it does not independently prove C127’s proposed Franklin identity.
The baptism names Édouard, born15March1823, baptized16March, Abraham Gendreau and Marie Pellerin of Montmagny. Census page2 line7 names Édouard Gendreau, menuisier,37, unmarried (n in column10), following Abraham Gendreau75, Marie Pellerin64 and Thomas Gendreau26, and preceding Herménégilde Gendreau23. Herménégilde’s original18December1837 baptism, born17December, gives the same parents and parish and independently corroborates the distinctive younger brother in the census household. Marie Virginie’s original10September1835 baptism gives the same parents; Virginie Gendreau25 occurs on line50 at the bottom of this census page, not beside the parents. Virginie is neighborhood corroboration only, not a claimed member of the immediate parental household.
Limitations: The1861 census does not explicitly label Édouard as son; that relationship is inferred from combined names, ages, parish and the independently documented sibling. The age column is printed age at next birthday; Édouard’s37 differs by one from38 at his next1861 birthday but fits completed age before15March1861. Herménégilde23 likewise fits completed age early1861 rather than next birthday24. Census ages are approximate; no exact age agreement is claimed under the printed rule. The census’s Thomas26 is not identified with a specific baptism in this report. Brief inspection of the full page and enlarged crop confirmed the unmarried column rather than relying on a tree’s marital claim. Local origin and unmarried status, not physical presence in Canada alone, underpin the exclusion.
Qualified1879 lead: Original28January1879 Montmagny M9 records Édouard Gendreau, carpenter, son of deceased Abraham Gendreau and deceased Marie Pelletier, marrying Marie Normand, widow of Jean Baptiste Patry. Pelletier differs from Pellerin in the1823 baptism and1861 census. The original surname is preserved, not silently corrected. NosOrigines2142660 and FamilySearchGWKR-76P associate the events, but those compiled links do not resolve this conflict. The1879 marriage is retained as qualified context and is NOT necessary to the counted1861 census exclusion. The asserted1912 death was not independently inspected and is not used.
