## Identification: Peter Plush

Pierre is probably the Peter Plush of Missouri and Michigan. He married at St. Charles, Missouri, on 19 February 1849 — recorded as Peter Gendron — Margaritha Grefe, before Jos Brunner, a Catholic priest. Collet's index to the St. Charles Borromeo marriage register names the groom's parents as Baptiste Gendron and Claire Chetinser, with the compiler's own alternative reading "(Chatagne?)" — a reading consistent with the proposed identification of Jean-Baptiste Gendron and Claire Chatigny, Pierre's parents. The original Borromeo marriage register for 1845–1892 is not filmed, so the parent naming rests on that compiled index rather than the register itself. The couple's daughter Aspasie, baptized at Borromeo on 1 May 1853, appears as Spizia Plush, aged 7 and born in Missouri, in Peter's 1860 Delta County, Michigan household — the same family under the anglicized surname. Claire Chatigny's 1839 petition identifies her husband as Jean-Baptiste Gendron dit Peloche, supporting the proposed connection between the surnames Peloche and Plush.

Peter was a labourer in 1860 and a farmer in 1880. Both censuses give Canadian birth, with ages 33 and 53 — about one year below Pierre's expected completed ages of 34 and 54 — and the 1880 census gives both his parents' birthplaces as Canada. His wife Margaret was also born in Canada. The 1860 household includes two older children born in Missouri and two younger children born in Michigan, and Zoa Grif, 17, born in Canada. Her similar surname suggests a connection to Margaret Grefe, but the census does not state their relationship. Peter declared naturalization at the St. Charles Circuit Court on 16 May 1854, the same county as his marriage.

The American family is traced in Missouri and Michigan until at least 1880. The identification remains likely because the parent-naming evidence rests on a compiled index with an uncertain maternal-surname reading. The original Borromeo marriage entry and the original 1854 St. Charles naturalization declaration are the next documents to inspect for independent identifying details. Their contents, rather than their retrieval alone, would determine whether the identification can be made solid.
