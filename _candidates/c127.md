## Biography

- François Jandron was born and baptized at Châteauguay on 6 September 1823, son of Pierre Jandron, a day labourer, and Marie Paré. [Baptism](#document-act-2628150)
- He is the proposed identity of Franklin Jondro of Missouri; the comparison uses Franklin's American records, the Châteauguay family and the other candidates in this register. [1850 census index](#document-source-002) · [1860 census index](#document-source-003) · [1880 census abstract](#document-source-004)


## Franklin in the American records

American census records identify a Canadian-born farmer with an established family in Missouri. In 1850, Franklin Johndro appears in St. Clair County with Phebe A. and their infant son Isaac D. In 1860, he appears with Polly A., identified in the U.S. Evidence Chain as Mary Ann Lord, and their children; his birthplace is recorded as "Montreal, Canada." The 1880 census again records a Canadian birthplace.

The later census entries for Franklin's children add specific evidence of a French-Canadian background. In 1900, Lewis Mitchell Johndrow's entry gives his father's birthplace as "Fr Canada," and Cordelia Tucker's gives "Canada (Fr)." In 1910, Isaac Johndrow and Parry Curnutt each have "Can. French" in the father's birthplace field. The 1920 returns for Nancy Pennington, Franklin Siegel Johndrow and Mary Detty separately record their father's mother tongue as French. The reports also conflict: several carry English qualifiers, Fannie's 1920 entry gives Germany and German, Cordelia's 1920 entry gives Missouri, and Parry's 1900 entry gives Kentucky. Read together with Franklin's own Canadian and Montréal birthplace reports, the explicit Canada-French and French-language entries support a French-Canadian origin.

## The Châteauguay record and the search for alternatives

The civil and parish counterparts of the Châteauguay register record François's birth and baptism on 6 September 1823 and name his parents as Pierre Jandron and Marie Paré — standardized by PRDH as Pierre Gendron and Marie Madeleine Paré ([LAFRANCE act 2628150](https://www.genealogiequebec.com/Membership/LAFRANCE/acte/2628150)). His parents married on 16 November 1818 at Caughnawaga (Sault-St-Louis); PRDH records three children for the couple — Louis (1819, died in infancy), an unnamed infant (1821), and François — and no later Québec marriage or burial for François himself. This birth year fits Franklin's lifetime ages, and Châteauguay is compatible with his reported Montréal-area origin. Jandron also resembles the American forms Johndro, Jondro, Jondra and Johndrow. These agreements support comparison, but no cited record expressly connects the given names François and Franklin.

The alternatives were located with PRDH and LAFRANCE and assessed by comparing original records. The documented search used GENDRON or GENDREAU and FRANCOIS, with similar spellings enabled, male baptism subjects, all parishes, and the years 1816–1830; it returned 30 acts, each matched to this baptism or to one of the 29 alternatives in the report's Appendix B (the register on this site lists every candidate the wider consolidation produced).

## Comparison with other candidates

Childhood burials identify candidates through their names and parents. Adult identities are traced through marriages, spouses, children, and census households. Each candidate's page sets out the records and conclusion. Cyrille's Vermont record, Régis's census identification, and Napoléon's census sequence remain likely eliminations.

## The older Châteauguay candidate

François Xavier Gendron, born 12 May and baptized 13 May 1815 at Châteauguay (PRDH individual 2377060; act 2626495), falls outside a reasonable age window. Measured against the census and draft ages, his birth would require Franklin to have misstated his age consistently by at least seven years, and it places his birth five years before the gravestone date. His later life has not been traced, but the age problem is enough on its own.

## Assessment of the proposed identity

Taken together, the American profile, the Châteauguay baptism and the documented comparisons support the 1823 François as the best-supported identification among the records examined. The argument is circumstantial: no cited record names Franklin's Canadian parents or directly joins his Missouri life to this baptism. The gravestone birth date and the remaining candidate identifications are unresolved.
