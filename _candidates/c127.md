Although there is no direct evidence linking them, there is a lot of indirect evidence that Franklin Jondro of Missouri was François Gendron, born and baptized at Châteauguay on 6 September 1823. This identification agrees with Franklin's American profile and the Châteauguay record. It is presented here the way the elimination report presents it — the American records and the Canadian record first, the comparison second — and it remains a proposal, not a proof.

## Franklin in the American records

American census records identify a Canadian-born farmer with an established family in Missouri. In 1850, Franklin Johndro appears in St. Clair County with Phebe A. and their infant son Isaac D. In 1860, he appears with Polly A., identified in the U.S. Evidence Chain as Mary Ann Lord, and their children; his birthplace is recorded as "Montreal, Canada." The 1880 census again records a Canadian birthplace.

The later census entries for Franklin's children add specific evidence of a French-Canadian background. In 1900, Lewis Mitchell Johndrow's entry gives his father's birthplace as "Fr Canada," and Cordelia Tucker's gives "Canada (Fr)." In 1910, Isaac Johndrow and Parry Curnutt each have "Can. French" in the father's birthplace field. The 1920 returns for Nancy Pennington, Franklin Siegel Johndrow and Mary Detty separately record their father's mother tongue as French. The reports also conflict: several carry English qualifiers, Fannie's 1920 entry gives Germany and German, Cordelia's 1920 entry gives Missouri, and Parry's 1900 entry gives Kentucky. Read together with Franklin's own Canadian and Montréal birthplace reports, the explicit Canada-French and French-language entries support a French-Canadian origin. They provide a basis for the Québec search independently of the proposed 1823 baptism, while neither their agreement nor their inconsistencies identify that baptism by themselves.

Franklin's reported ages vary. The four lifetime reports imply birth years from approximately 1822 to 1826; his gravestone instead gives 22 July 1820. The 1863 age is taken from the draft registration card itself.

| Record | Recorded information | Approximate birth year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1850 census | Age 24 | 1826 |
| 1860 census | Age 38 | 1822 |
| 1863 draft registration | Age 38 | 1825 |
| 1880 census | Age 57 | 1823 |
| Gravestone | Born 22 July 1820 | 1820 |

Extending the earliest and latest lifetime estimates by four years gives a search window of 1816–1830 — the window this register covers. The gravestone's full birth date conflicts with the Châteauguay baptism and remains part of the comparison. A contemporary baptism is strong evidence of the birth of the child it names, but that does not by itself establish that the child later became Franklin.

Franklin died in Hickory County, Missouri, on 13 April 1886. His daughter Nancy Ann's death certificate names him as her father.

## The Châteauguay record and the search for alternatives

The civil and parish counterparts of the Châteauguay register record François's birth and baptism on 6 September 1823 and name his parents as Pierre Jandron and Marie Paré — standardized by PRDH as Pierre Gendron and Marie Madeleine Paré ([LAFRANCE act 2628150](https://www.genealogiequebec.com/Membership/LAFRANCE/acte/2628150)). His parents married on 16 November 1818 at Caughnawaga (Sault-St-Louis); PRDH records three children for the couple — Louis (1819, died in infancy), an unnamed infant (1821), and François — and no later Québec marriage or burial for François himself. This birth year fits Franklin's lifetime ages, and Châteauguay is compatible with his reported Montréal-area origin. Jandron also resembles the American forms Johndro, Jondro, Jondra and Johndrow. These agreements support comparison, but no cited record expressly connects the given names François and Franklin.

The alternatives were located with PRDH and LAFRANCE, then compared on the register images and other evidence for each candidate; the argument rests on those records and their identifying details, rather than on PRDH's reconstructed family links. The documented search used GENDRON or GENDREAU and FRANCOIS, with similar spellings enabled, male baptism subjects, all parishes, and the years 1816–1830; it returned 30 acts, each matched to this baptism or to one of the 29 alternatives in the report's Appendix B (the register on this site lists every candidate the wider consolidation produced). The list establishes what this search returned, not that every possible Canadian-born namesake has been found.

## What the comparison establishes

Many alternatives are distinguished by a childhood death naming the same parents. Others are followed from baptism to marriage through their parents' names, then through a repeated spouse, widowhood or children; those continuing families are compared with Franklin's Missouri family. A Canadian event alone does not preclude later travel, and naming a father in a child's baptism does not necessarily establish that he attended it.

The strength of these links varies. Some depend on family correlations; one attributes a burial despite differences in the second name and age; Cyrille's Vermont continuation uses a published account; Régis is counted as eliminated on an 1851 census sighting alone — the thinnest of these — while Napoléon's continuing adult identity is supported by census correlations. These distinctions are kept with the records rather than combined into a claim that every alternative is conclusively eliminated — the register still contains alternatives that are unresolved or qualified.

## The older Châteauguay candidate

François Xavier Gendron, born 12 May and baptized 13 May 1815 at Châteauguay (PRDH individual 2377060; act 2626495), falls outside a reasonable age window. Measured against the census and draft ages, his birth would require Franklin to have misstated his age consistently by at least seven years, and it places his birth five years before the gravestone date. His later life has not been traced, but the age problem is enough on its own.

## Assessment of the proposed identity

Taken together, the American profile, the Châteauguay baptism and the documented comparisons support the 1823 François as the best-supported identification among the records examined. The argument is circumstantial: no cited record names Franklin's Canadian parents or directly joins his Missouri life to this baptism. The gravestone discrepancy, the qualified comparisons and the older uneliminated candidate remain part of the assessment. DNA evidence is not relied on here.
