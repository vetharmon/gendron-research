## Follow-up — 16 November 2026

The original [10 January 1843 St-Hugues remarriage](https://www.genealogiequebec.com/Membership/LAFRANCE/acte/13499521), including its continuation (d1p_03790961.jpg and d1p_03790962.jpg), confirms Jacques Gendron as widower of Geneviève Goulet. He married Louise Chartier, daughter of Jean Baptiste Chartier and the late Louise Touron. Thomas is not named in either portion of the act.

Two adult Thomas marriage leads were checked in the detailed indexes and belong to different families: [Trois-Pistoles, 7 October 1856](https://www.genealogiequebec.com/Membership/LAFRANCE/acte/13400845), names Augustin Gendreau and Julie Gagné; [St-Pierre-de-la-Rivière-du-Sud, 25 February 1862](https://www.genealogiequebec.com/Membership/LAFRANCE/acte/13564446), names Abraham Gendreau and Marie Pellerin. These are index-level parentage checks, not original-image readings.

No later record has yet been connected to Thomas. The prison lead remains unassigned and his assessment remains **Needs work**.

## Prison archive follow-up — 16 November 2026

BAnQ’s own prison database was searched for Thomas Gendron and returned one entry. It confirms age 22, origin Canada, admission 24 April 1848 and discharge 28 April upon payment of a fine. The committal was by **W. K. McCord**, with the charge recorded as “Loose idle etc” and sentence “One week H.L. (Hard Labour).” Occupation, height and complexion fields are blank. The remarks describe him as single, unable to read or write, intemperate, and of good conduct in gaol. These are the index’s historical descriptions, not independent conclusions about C178.

The precise reference is **E17,S1,P5, container 1960-01-036/1573, volume 5, folio 10**. [BAnQ register catalogue and digitized-volume link](https://advitam.banq.qc.ca/notice/243739). The catalogue lists 365 images, but the image viewer timed out; the original folio remains uninspected. [BAnQ prison database](https://www2.banq.qc.ca/archives/genealogie_histoire_familiale/ressources/bd/recherche.html?id=PRISON_2020).

The next identifying evidence would be a specific residence, relative, employer or other independently traceable associate in the original register or related committal/court paperwork. Name, age and Canada alone do not identify this prisoner as C178. Even a secure 1848 identification would not itself eliminate him as Franklin in Missouri in 1850. **Needs work remains unchanged.**

## Court-file lead — 16 November 2026

A search of BAnQ’s Thémis 2 database (D–H surnames) for Thomas Gendron returned three descriptions. The **24 April 1848** deposition and conviction identifies Thomas as a **labourer of Québec City**. Police constable **William Brown** complained that he was drunk and obstructing people in the street. The exact date and name strongly connect this court description with the prison admission, although the original court document has not yet been inspected.

**Reference:** Archives nationales à Québec, **TL31,S1,SS1**, container **1960-01-357/147**, document **132575**. [BAnQ Thémis 2 search](https://www2.banq.qc.ca/archives/genealogie_histoire_familiale/ressources/bd/recherche.html?id=THEMIS_2_DH&2=Gendron&3=Thomas&4=&5=&6=&7=&8=&9=&10=).

This adds occupation and city of residence to the prison lead. The description gives no parents or specific birthplace, so identity with C178 remains unestablished. The other results concern a Saint-Raymond physician in 1898 and a deposition in 1902; neither has been attached to this candidate. Next: inspect document 132575 for address, age, origin or relatives. **Needs work unchanged.**

## Saint-Hugues household and census coverage — 25 November 2026

The [original 1861 census](https://data2.collectionscanada.gc.ca/1861/jpg/4108658_00281.jpg) places **Jacques Gendron, 61**, and **Louise Chartier, 46**, together at Saint-Hugues, Bagot, with five younger Gendrons. The entry is on **printed page 130, handwritten page 6, lines 9–15**, reel **C-1263**. Both adults are marked married; Jacques’s occupation cell is blank. The couple and locality correspond to the family established by Jacques’s already-read **10 January 1843 remarriage**. [Household abstract](#document-census-1861-saint-hugues-jacques)

Neither **Joseph** nor **Thomas** is listed in that household, and its absent-family columns are blank. This establishes a later location for their father and stepmother. It does not establish either son’s death or residence elsewhere.

The [official LAC coverage guide](https://www.canada.ca/fr/bibliotheque-archives/collection/aide-recherche/genealogie-histoire-famille/recensements/avant-confederation/1851/districts/canada-est-quebec.html) lists **Saint-Hugues, subdistrict 408 of Saint-Hyacinthe district 31, as missing for 1851**. The earlier unsuccessful index search therefore cannot establish that either son was absent from that parish. The 1861 manual review covered the **53 nominal pages numbered 125–177** in the Saint-Hugues sequence; it found no entry identifying either adult son. This remains a bounded search result. **Needs work is unchanged.**

## Parental inventory located — 27 November 2026

The official [Montréal inventory index](#document-brodeur-gendron-goulet-1839-index) supplies a new, precise family target: **Jacques Gendron**, a farmer at **Saint-Hugues**, and **Geneviève Goulet, deceased**, dated **1 April 1839**, before notary **Timothée Brodeur**, **CN602,S14**. The couple and locality match the family already documented in the parish records. The inventory date falls after Geneviève’s indexed 1837 burial and before Jacques’s verified 1843 remarriage.

The [notarial catalogue](#document-brodeur-catalogue-1837-1840) narrows retrieval to **acquisition 1983-09-067, container 551, September 1837–September 1840, microfilm 9324**. Its separate chronological répertoire is **container 1168**, covering **1826–1861**, minutes **1–3679**; **1–3679 is the repertoire’s range, not this act’s number**.

**The inventory remains unread.** The index gives no children, ages, guardians or heirs. Joseph and Thomas would still have been minors, so the complete opening representation clauses and any continuation are the priority for testing their presence in the family. No conclusion about either son’s survival, death or later identity follows from the catalogue entry. The digital viewer presented a verification barrier during this pass; no alternate image route was used. **Both C143 and C178 remain Needs work.**
