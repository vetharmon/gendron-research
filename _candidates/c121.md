## Research assessment

Baptism/index act: 2878095
CURRENT STATUS: Eliminated — original images verified
Upgraded after new original parental-name evidence and the child's baptism were inspected. Original 21 July 1823 Cap-St-Ignace baptism, act 2878095, d1p_10051108.jpg bottom right: Charles Eugene, born previous day, son of Charles Gendreau and Constance Cecile Fournier. Original 22 February 1848 Cap-St-Ignace marriage, act 13400802, M7 left page, names Eugene Gendreau, adult son of a deceased Gendreau father and deceased Cecile Constance Fournier, marrying Cesarie Landry, daughter of Joseph Landry and Solange Richard.
Transcription clarification: the previous log confidently read the father's given name in the 1848 marriage as Francois. Enlarged reinspection is less secure and allows a Charles reading; neither is silently substituted as certain. The new original 6 November 1820 Cap-St-Ignace parental marriage, act 5716116, d1p_10051055.jpg upper left, explicitly labels the couple in the margin as Francois Charles Gendreau and Cecile Constance Fournier. The original identifies that same parental couple, giving an independent basis for the Charles/Francois usage whichever reading of the low-resolution 1848 word is accepted. PRDH profile 653126/family 140251 corroborates the full paternal name; the compound is present in the original marriage margin, not merely supplied by the database.
Original 5 January 1849 Cap-St-Ignace baptism, act 3236118, d1p_10060452.jpg B2 upper left: Marie Cesarie born previous day, legitimate daughter of Eugene Gendreau, journalier, and Cesarie Landry. The father is explicitly absent; his attendance or signature is not claimed. The linked parentage, distinct February 1848 marriage and January 1849 child support exclusion under the previously applied 1848-1849 separate-family criterion, given Franklin's different wife and infant son in Missouri in 1850. This entry does not claim a verified post-1850 event for Eugene. An Ancestry compiled-tree lead suggests a Michigan Johndrow identity and 1870 death, but no original Michigan record or census was verified in this pass, and that lead is not used to inflate the evidence.
