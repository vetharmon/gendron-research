## Biography

Alexandre Gendron was baptized on 22 June 1820 at Ste-Anne-de-la-Pérade (Ste-Anne), the son of Louis Gendron and Marguerite Gervais, one of twelve children of that couple baptized there. No later record has been securely identified in the marriage, burial, godparent and census searches recorded below. He was not found in his widowed mother's 1851 household at Ste-Anne or his brother Hubert's 1861 household at St-Casimir.

## Documents

- **[Baptism, 22 June 1820 — LAFRANCE act 2936933](https://www.genealogiequebec.com/Membership/LAFRANCE/acte/2936933)** — His baptism at Ste-Anne-de-la-Pérade. It is the only record of him in his file.

A compiled profile reports an Alexandre Gendron and Henriette Perrault as sponsors at Edmund Gendron’s 1856 baptism in Ontario. The original has not been inspected. The separate Colville lead now has an official archival biography, discussed below; neither lead establishes C065’s identity.

## A marriage witness to investigate — 21 November 2026

The county museum’s [modern typed transcription of Reverend Charles Manson’s marriages](https://archive.org/details/reverend-charles-manson-baptisms-1848-1888), [page 1](https://archive.org/download/reverend-charles-manson-baptisms-1848-1888/Reverend%20Charles%20%20Manson%20Marriages%201848-1888.pdf#page=1), records **Michael Gendron and Vanda Judson**, dated **22 January 1851**, with witnesses **Anson J Brown and Alex. Gendron**. **Bastard** appears in the residence column for Michael, followed by “do” for Vanda. It is not a stated ceremony location or a stated residence for Alex.

The transcription page was visually inspected; **the underlying manuscript has not been read**. It gives no ages, parents or relationship between the witness and groom. Preserve **Alex.** as written rather than expanding it into a proved Alexandre identity, and preserve **Vanda** rather than silently changing the bride’s name to Amanda. The groom is also a comparison lead for [C039, Michel Archange Gendron](../c039/), but neither man is identified as one of the Québec brothers.

The next step is the underlying Manson register entry, followed by independent records that could identify the groom and witness. This is a specific new witness lead; **C065 remains Needs work**.

## An original census comparison for Alex. — 21 November 2026

The **[1851 personal census for Bastard Township, Part 1](https://data2.collectionscanada.gc.ca/1851_pdf/e095/e002356142.pdf)**, Canada West, Leeds County, handwritten page 19, printed pages 37–38 (sheets e002356142 and e002356143), was inspected. At **line 39** it records **Alexander Gendron, farmer, born Canada East, Roman Catholic, age 21 next birthday**. This supplies a precise local person to compare with the **Alex. Gendron** witness in the Manson marriage transcription.

At **lines 29–31** on the same page are **Michel Gendron, shoemaker, 29**, **Vanda Gendron, 21**, and **Moses S. Gendron, 1**, male. Michel is recorded as born in Canada East and Roman Catholic; Vanda as born in Canada West and E. Methodist. The Michel/Vanda pairing and Bastard residence strongly correlate with the marriage entry, but **Alexander is separated from these three entries by the Wing entries and is a non-family male resident in the Robins household**. The census does not state a sibling connection between him and Michel, and it gives no parents.

Alexander's recorded age is younger than expected from C065's 1820 baptism. Preserve this discrepancy, the distinction between **Alex.** in the marriage transcription and **Alexander** in the census, and the possibility of unrelated namesakes. The next useful proof would independently identify Alexander's parents or connect him to the established family of **Louis Gendron and Marguerite Gervais**. The reported 1856 sponsor and Henriette Perrault lead remains open; **Needs work is unchanged**.

## Western namesake — archival comparison, 21 November 2026

The **[Hudson's Bay Company Archives biography of Alexandre/Alexis Gendron](https://www.gov.mb.ca/chc/archives/_assets/docs/hbca/biographical/g/gendron_alexandre.pdf)** supplies firmer documentation for the western lead previously known from a family tree. It identifies **St-Michel, Yamaska**, records entry into service in **1832**, and reports **age 25 in both 1835 and 1836**. Its employment chronology lists **Fort Colville district** assignments as a middleman in **1850–1851** and a miller in **1851–1854**. These are outfit-year headings; the sheet also notes retirement there in **1853**.

One cited original has now been recovered and read: **[B.239/g/31, 1851–1852 accounts, PDF image 166](https://pam.minisisinc.com/DIGITALOBJECTS/Access/HBCA%20Microfilm/1M807/B239-G-31.pdf#page=166)**, manuscript page **77**, account folio **172**.
