### The 1810 marriage still needs a name bridge

The [5 March 1810 Châteauguay marriage copy](#document-chateauguay-marriage-1810-03-05-copy) records **Jean-Baptiste Jeandron**, adult son of **Jean-Baptiste Jeandron and Françoise Lafontaine**, marrying **Marguerite Lamagdeleine**, adult daughter of **Louis Lamagdeleine and Marie Daragon**. Both fathers witnessed. **François is not given as the groom’s name.**

The date and maternal surname make this a useful comparison, but the typed copy does not prove that this Jean-Baptiste is the François named in the later baptisms. Nor does Marie Anne **Dadagon** at the 1813 baptism establish that she was the grandmother Marie Daragon. Keep both readings as written and seek the contemporary marriage or another act explicitly connecting the names.

### A childhood burial with two discrepancies

A [21 November 1813 Châteauguay burial copy](#document-chateauguay-francois-burial-1813-11-21-copy) records **François**, dead the previous day, aged **three and a half**, son of **François Jeandron and Magdeleine Lamagdeleine**. The boy baptized in May 1811 would instead have been **two and a half**, and his baptism names **Marguerite**. Both the age and maternal given name differ. The burial may concern that child, but the copies alone do not resolve the identification. It cannot be the burial of Amand or Louis, who had not yet been born.

The most useful new family tests are an adult record for **Marguerite, born 1813**, or **Luce, born 1823**, naming parents or other relatives, and a contemporary source resolving the **Jean-Baptiste/François** comparison. No adult record has yet been securely linked to either sister. **C034 and C195 remain Needs work.**
