## Biography

Michel Archange Gendron was born on 3 April 1818 and baptized the next day at Ste-Anne-de-la-Pérade (Ste-Anne), the son of Louis Gendron and Marguerite Gervais. No later record has been securely connected to his baptism. A compiled Ancestry profile for Cornelius George Gendron names his father as Michel Archange Gendron (1818–1874), with Amanda Vanda Judson as the mother. This is an Ontario identification lead. A municipal cemetery transcription also records Delilah, daughter of Mitchell and Amanda Gendron, who died on 10 December 1864, aged six years eleven months. Neither source identifies the father as the son of Louis Gendron and Marguerite Gervais. C039 remains possible.

Searches of Québec marriages and burials, Canadian census indexes for 1851, 1861 and 1871, and US census indexes for 1850 and 1860 did not establish a later record for this baptismal candidate. Neither Michel nor Alexandre was found in their mother's 1851 household or their brother Hubert's 1861 household. These are search results, not proof of death or emigration.

## Ontario identification conflict

A. C. Osborne’s *Old Penetanguishene*, in *Pioneer Papers*, No. 6 (1917), [printed pages 143–144](https://electriccanadian.com/history/ontario/pioneerpapers.pdf#page=351), gives the tanner’s birthplace as **St-Martin, Québec**. It connects him to Smith’s Falls and Lyn near Brockville, to a wife named Miss Judson, and to sons C. G., Sidney, Edmond, Michel, Vincent and Anthony. It records his death in September 1874 and his wife’s in 1896. The preceding page names Charles Gendron, father of Carolus, as Michel’s half-brother.

The St-Martin birthplace conflicts with the 1818 Ste-Anne-de-la-Pérade baptism. This is a concrete reason to question the compiled-tree identification. Osborne does not name Michel’s parents, and his account was published decades after Michel’s death. The reported move to Penetanguishene about 1835 also needs checking against the family chronology. The Ontario tanner cannot yet be used to eliminate C039.

## Later Ontario evidence

The original 1871 census of Tiny records Michael Gendron, 50, born in Québec, with Amanda, 39, and their children. John William Gendron’s 1909 death certificate names his parents as Michel Gendron and Amanda Judson and gives Michel’s birthplace as “Ste. Ann’s, Que.” This supports the Ontario lead, but does not identify Ste-Anne-de-la-Pérade uniquely or name Michel’s parents. The reported St-Martin origin and conflicting 1871/1874 death dates remain unresolved. **Possible elimination** is the current assessment.
