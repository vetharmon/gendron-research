## Research assessment

Baptism/index act: 4079947
CURRENT STATUS: Eliminated — original images verified
Conclusion: Pierre, born6April1826 and baptized7April at Montmagny St-Thomas, son of Pierre Gendreau and Marie Gamache, is correlated with the9August1852 Sainte-Luce Frenchville groom of Barbe Caron and the1860 northern Maine Peter/Babe family. The original marriage supplies the parent-pair bridge; the original census supplies a continuing family with children born in Maine. This separate family chronology conflicts with Franklin Jondro's documented Missouri wife and children in1850 and1860. This is a reasoned combined-evidence exclusion, not independent proof that C127 is Franklin.
The1852 original describes Pierre as fils majeur of deceased Pierre Gendreau and deceased Marie Gamache; no former spouse is named. He resides at St François, while the parents are associated with Cap St Ignace, Bas Canada. Barbe is a minor daughter of Joseph Caron and Emilie Nadeau, St François. Joseph Caron and Paschal Gendreau witness the act. The exact parental pair and first name connect the adult to the1826 baptism. The nearby Cap St Ignace origin is not represented as an exact Montmagny parish match. Paschal's witness presence is compatible with the independently followed C076 brother (same parents), but the act does not explicitly call the witness his brother.
The1860 original census page61 top six lines records Peter Gendreau36, farmer, bornCanada; Babe25, bornMaine; Julie6, Sophie4, Louison3 and Amos8/12, all bornMaine. Spousal/child relationships are inferred from household order, names and the1852 marriage; the1860 form has no relationship column. The given-name forms Pierre/Peter and Barbe/Babe are retained. The census age36 differs by two years from34 calculated from6April1826. FamilySearch's public tree gives7April1822 and Cap-Saint-Ignace for his birth; that tree claim is not adopted. Original baptism gives1826 and bornpreviousday. No exact census-age match is claimed. The census alone would not establish the1826 baptism link; the contemporary marriage parents are essential. The baptism, marriage and continuing household together support this exclusion despite those discrepancies.
